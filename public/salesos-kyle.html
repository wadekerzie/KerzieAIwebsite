What Kyle's Sales OS does

Talk through your deals; it turns them into follow-ups, CRM updates and forecast notes, drafted and waiting for your yes.

What it does for you

  • Before a call: prep on the account, ready when you sit down.
  • After a call: the action items pulled out and the follow-up drafted.
  • Every morning: a brief with your moves and the drafts waiting on you.
  • Every Monday: a team view for your one-on-ones and roll-ups.
  • Forecast notes: drafted from what it can trace, and it says so when it isn't sure.
  • Nothing leaves your desk without your yes.

Start here

Do these on this Mac, in order. About 15 minutes, plus a few for the Terminal install.

  1. Download your setup file.
    Download sales-os-setup.md
  2. In Documents, make a folder named Kyle's Sales OS. Move the setup file into it. Inside it, make a folder named my-notes and put your voice memo transcripts there.
  3. One command in Terminal, once. Press Command + Space, type Terminal, press Return. Paste this, press Return, then click Install in the window that appears. If it says the tools are already installed, that's fine.
    xcode-select --install
  4. Open Claude, click the Code tab, click Select folder, and pick Kyle's Sales OS. If it asks whether you trust the folder, say yes.
  5. Copy this, paste it into Claude, and press Return. It reads your transcripts first, then asks only what they don't answer.
    Read sales-os-setup.md and set up my Sales OS. Start with Step 0. My voice memo transcripts are in the my-notes folder: read them first and answer every question you can from them, then ask me only what they don't cover.

The 16 things your OS is built to do

A reference list in two groups: what it does, and why it stays reliable. Some switch on in week one, the rest as you're ready.

What it does

  1. SetupIt interviews you about how you actually sell, then builds itself around your answers. You don't configure it.
  2. Voice and meeting captureTranscripts and voice memos land in your folder. It pulls the action items, files them, and starts the work. It stops at sending, posting, changing the CRM, spending, or contacting anyone.
  3. GuardrailsIt drafts and stages. Emails, CRM changes and anything that leaves your desk wait for your yes.
  4. The paneOne screen: your moves, its moves, what's scheduled, and your weekly rhythm.
  5. The night shiftOptional, from week two. Each night it reconciles the day's work and lines up tomorrow's prep.
  6. Dated work goes on the calendarIf you say yes, anything with a date that needs you becomes a calendar event with the full instructions inside.
  7. ConnectorsEmail, calendar, CRM, meeting transcripts and documents: only what your company approves, each one added with your yes.
  8. Routines with receiptsJobs run on a schedule. Each one writes a receipt: worked, nothing to do, needs you, or failed. A run with no receipt gets flagged.
  9. It checks before it tells youIt checks the real thing before telling you something is true. Forecasts and reports carry only what it can trace. When it isn't sure, it says so.
  10. One placeThe folder lives on your Mac, or only on storage your company approved. Company data never goes to a personal account.

Why it stays reliable

  1. MemoryEvery session opens by reading one short briefing. Older detail moves to deeper folders. Nothing is deleted. You never re-explain your job.
  2. Stays leanThe briefing has a size ceiling. A weekly checkup flags anything that grew and moves it down a tier.
  3. Costs stay lowRoutine work runs on smaller, cheaper models. Judgment and anything that goes up the chain gets the top model.
  4. SkillsThe first time something works, the steps get written down, and it runs the same way from then on.
  5. SecretsAnything sensitive lives in one private folder. It's never printed into chat and never leaves your Mac.
  6. PortableYour rules and memory are plain files, so another AI tool your company approves can run the same OS with a few file names changed.
