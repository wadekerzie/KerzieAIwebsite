04 · The insight

Home repair is a latent need. That breaks every ad playbook.

A homeowner who saves our number has converted, and produces zero signal until something breaks. The industry buys clicks and measures immediate response in a category where the trigger is random.

Our best month ran on zero ad spend: one person explaining the service face to face. The raise is about systematizing that education. The winning motion is education and density, not click harvesting.

05 · Market

Two metros, $1,400 to $2,300 a year per household, attacked subdivision by subdivision.

The Denver-Aurora MSA alone holds 1.27M households, $1.8B to $2.9B in annual maintenance spend. Collin County TX adds 1.3M people in the #2 US county for population gain. Across roughly 130M US households the category runs $180-300B.

Near-term target is deliberately small and density-first: a few thousand habitual households across three markets. Route density and word of mouth compound at the subdivision level.

06 · Business model

20% of every completed job. The household is the asset.

Average ticket in the $400 range; our take is about $80. The first job roughly breaks even, and we say that out loud, because a household needs seven-plus repairs a year and every repeat compounds for us instead of being resold as a lead.

AI runs the coordination, dispatch, and follow-up. That is why two people run two markets, and why a new market costs a phone number, a zip configuration, and contractor onboarding.

07 · Competition

We are not a better Angi. We are simpler than a phone call.

Angi and Thumbtack sell the homeowner's request to multiple contractors and resell the relationship every time. Membership services ask for commitment up front. Our real competitor is the status quo: call someone you know, or put it off.

Defensibility is the homeowner habit, neighborhood density, and the contractor bench, and the raise is pointed at all three.

Where this goes

The plan above does not depend on any of this.

It is worth saying out loud anyway, because it is why the shape of the thing matters more than the size of it today.

Agentic AI arrives on the phone.

The assistant in a homeowner's pocket stops answering questions and starts doing things. It books. It compares. It follows up. When that lands, the question stops being which app a homeowner opens. It becomes which businesses their agent can actually transact with.

GotaGuy is already in that format.

Nothing to install. No account, no portal, no password to recover. A thread. We built that interface for people who did not want software, and it happens to be the one an agent can already use.

Then the work itself.

Coordination is the part we automate today. Some of the repair automates later, and that timeline is anyone's guess. In either world the durable asset is the same: the household relationship and the bench that serves it. Dispatch outlives whoever, or whatever, ends up holding the tools.

Thesis, not forecast. None of it is in the plan, the numbers, or the use of funds above.
