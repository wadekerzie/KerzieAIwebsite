Warm Callback Sprint: Talking Points

Talking points, not a script. You know the message. You say it in your own words.

Calling Tuesday September 15 and Wednesday September 16. Jane calls Texas, Juvi calls Colorado. Meetings book on Wednesday and Thursday. Working sheets: the Callback 9-15 tab and the Open slots tab.

The sprint in three lines.

  • 102 businesses are open to call. Jane has 39 in Texas, Juvi 63 in Colorado.
  • $5 an hour, plus $50 for every meeting that actually happens.
  • Two or three tries per business across the two days. Voicemail only after two tries, and a voicemail is never the end of the story.

● The whole call in one line.

You are looking for the person who reviewed the email. That is the person you ask for 15 minutes. Everything else is just conversation. Say it naturally, in your own words. Do not read anything on this page to them.

Why we are calling

  • Every business on your list already got Wade's email. It showed how that business shows up when a person asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity for a business like theirs in their city. ● Every email also named a competitor in their industry that does show up. You can say that.
  • This is a friendly follow-up, not a cold call. The door is open. You are continuing something, not starting it.
  • The goal is 15 minutes on the calendar, Wednesday or Thursday. Wade's calendar for Texas, Aaron's for Colorado. That is the win.
  • In person first, screen share second. ● Wade or Aaron walks in, flips the laptop open, and shows them their own website. That is easier than getting anyone to commit to a web call. If they only want a screen share, let that be their decision.
  • You earn $50 when that meeting happens. Whoever made the booking call, and whether or not Wade sells anything.

What to know before you dial

Open the Callback 9-15 tab and read three columns before every call.

  • SPOKE TO is the person you already talked to there. Say this name out loud. It is the reason nobody asks "who are you."
  • DECISION MAKER is the person Wade emailed. That is who you ask for. If it is blank, ask: "Who can I speak with that would have reviewed that email?"
  • ● EMAIL ON CALL is the address they gave us. Say it back: "we sent some information over to info@..." They gave us that address, and it is usually not on their website, so saying it proves this is not a cold call.
  • If SPOKE TO is blank, lead with the email address. If both are blank, "I called recently and we sent some information over" is still true.
  • Never say a date. Say "recently sent you some information." It is true for every row, and you never have to look anything up.

The five things to get across

Say these in your own words, in this order. Cover all five and the call was done well, whatever words you used.

  1. You called before, and you spoke to a real person there. Give that name, or the email address. "I called recently and spoke with Sherry. Wade sent some information over to Steve. Is Steve around?"
  2. Wade sent information about how the business shows up in AI search. ● The line, in Wade's words: "Your website isn't built to be findable when people search with AI, and we can help you fix that." Not "built for AI search." It is not technical: AI reads websites differently, and theirs is not set up to be found.
  3. Wade is local, and he would like 15 minutes to show it. ● "We're a local tech company. This is what we do. It's very straightforward. We can show you in 15 minutes. We can stop by. What time works for you?"
  4. Offer two open times from the Open slots tab. ● Wednesday and Thursday. Two choices is easy. Four makes people panic. If they offer a different time, take it. The goal is the meeting.
  5. Confirm the email and the phone, and say Wade confirms today. Read both back out loud. A booking with a wrong email is not a booking.

Colorado only: Aaron is right here in the Denver metro and can come by in person. Say that. It is your strongest line, because it is true and local. Wade takes the Colorado screen share meetings if Aaron cannot.

What they might say, and the idea behind your answer

1. "What is this regarding?"

Idea: answer plainly, then ask in the same breath. Do not explain the technology.

Example, not a line to read

Wade sent the owner information about how the business shows up in AI search. I have his calendar here. I am calling to set 15 minutes for him to come by and show it. Is [decision maker] in today?

2. "Just send it again."

Idea: a yes to a resend, not a no. Take the address, then still ask for the 15 minutes.

Examples

Wade already sent it. I can have him resend it today. What is the best address?

Can I also hold 15 minutes for him Wednesday or Thursday? If the email answers everything, you can cancel.

3. "Not interested," from the front desk

Idea: the front desk is not the decision maker. Be gracious, end the call, try once more at a different time of day.

Example

Understood. Thank you. I will make a note.

4. "Not interested," from the decision maker

Idea: final. No second try, no persuading. Thank them and log it.

Example

Understood. Thank you for your time.

5. "Call me later," or "She is not in right now."

Idea: a good outcome. Get a real day and a real time, write it down, and call back then. ● The list is short enough that you keep track of these yourself.

Examples

Of course. What day and what time is good for you? I will call you then.

What time today or tomorrow is the best time to catch her?

6. "We already have a company that does this."

Idea: do not argue and do not compare. AI search is new, and most owners have never seen their own business in it. Wade shows them.

Examples

That is good to hear. This is about AI search specifically, which is very new.

Wade can show you in 15 minutes what your business looks like when somebody asks ChatGPT.

7. "How much does it cost?"

Idea: no price on this call, ever. Wade shows first and covers cost inside the 15 minutes. That is the honest answer, and it keeps the call short.

Examples

Wade goes over that with you in the meeting, after he shows you where you stand.

He wants you to see it first. That part takes 15 minutes.

8. "A visit is hard right now."

Idea: never lose a meeting over the format. Move to the screen share, same two times.

Example

Wade can do the same 15 minutes on a screen share. He shows you, and you decide. Would [slot A] or [slot B] work on a screen?

● 9. "Neither of those work, but we could do Friday at 4."

Idea: take it. A time they picked is better than a time we picked. Write it in the CLIENT-PROPOSED TIMES section of the Open slots tab. Next week only if they insist, and then write that down too: meetings set far out get delayed and cancelled, so we want them as soon as possible.

Example

Friday at 4 works. I will get that on his calendar. Is this the best number and email for the confirmation?

● 10. Texas only, today: "Can he come this afternoon?"

Idea: yes. Wade can be there any time between 1:00 and 5:00 today. Ask what time works best and write it down.

Words to use, words to avoid

Use these

  • AI search
  • shows up, does not show up
  • discoverable, findable
  • ● "a local tech company, this is what we do"
  • 15 minutes
  • show you on his screen
  • ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity by name
  • Kerzie AI, when asked who you are with

Avoid these

  • SEO
  • built for (say "built to be findable")
  • AI assistant
  • any price or any number
  • any promise about results or rankings
  • the date the email went out
  • "they cannot find you" (say "does not come up")

The rules in one place

  • Work the tab top to bottom. The rows are in order for a reason.
  • Skip any row Jordan already called. His callback column is filled in.
  • Two or three tries, then stop. Different times of day. Never a fourth. ● If the person is out, ask when to catch them and call back at that time. Do not leave a voicemail and call it done.
  • Voicemail only after two tries. Fifteen seconds: your name, Kerzie AI, we sent some information over, we help businesses be findable with AI search, we would like to show you how to fix your website, your Quo number. Never a second voicemail.
  • ● Row 20, Hunt Family Dentistry: skip it. No email on record, so there is nothing to follow up on.
  • A decision maker's no ends it. Log DM-NO. Nobody calls again.
  • A front-desk no allows one more try at a different time of day. Log GK-NO.
  • Log every attempt with a result code, including the ones nobody answered. An unlogged call never happened.
  • Honor an opt-out immediately, with no second ask.

Booking checklist

A booked row is not complete until all of this is in the sheet.

  • Result code: BOOKED-IN or BOOKED-WEB.
  • Business name.
  • Contact name, the person in the meeting.
  • Phone number, read back out loud.
  • Email address, read back out loud.
  • ● The day and time, written on the Open slots tab: in the BOOKED column next to the slot, or in CLIENT-PROPOSED TIMES if they picked their own time.

Tell them Wade confirms today. Wade or Aaron sends the invite the same day.
