Warm Callback Sprint: Talking Points

Talking points, not a script. You know the message. You say it in your own words.

Tuesday September 15 and Wednesday September 16. Jane calls Texas, Juvi calls Colorado. Working sheet: the Callback 9-15 tab.

The sprint in three lines.

  • 102 businesses are open to call. Jane has 39 in Texas, Juvi 63 in Colorado.
  • $5 an hour, plus $50 for every meeting that actually happens.
  • Three attempts per business across the two days. One voicemail only, on attempt two.

Why we are calling

  • Every business on your list already got Wade's email. It showed how that business shows up when a person asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity for a business like theirs in their city.
  • This is a friendly follow-up, not a cold call. The door is open. You are continuing something, not starting it.
  • The goal is 15 minutes on the calendar. Wade's calendar for Texas, Aaron's for Colorado. That is the win.
  • In person first, screen share second. Offer the visit. If a visit is hard, offer the screen. Both are good.
  • You earn $50 when that meeting happens. Whoever made the booking call, and whether or not Wade sells anything.

What to know before you dial

Open the Callback 9-15 tab and read two columns before every call.

  • SPOKE TO is the person a caller already talked to there. Say this name out loud. It is the reason nobody asks "who are you."
  • DECISION MAKER is the person Wade emailed. That is who you ask for.
  • If SPOKE TO is blank, there is no prior call on record. Go straight to the email.
  • Never say a date. Say "recently sent you some information." It is true for every row, and you never have to look anything up.

The five things to get across

Say these in your own words, in this order. Cover all five and the call was done well, whatever words you used.

  1. You called before, and you spoke to a real person there. Give that name. It turns a strange call into a familiar one.
  2. Wade sent the decision maker information about how the business shows up in AI search. Be specific: how they show up when someone asks ChatGPT for a dentist in Aurora, or a restaurant in McKinney. Use their category and their city.
  3. Wade is local, and he would like 15 minutes to show it on his screen. He shows. They decide.
  4. Offer two open times. Wade posts four Texas slots each morning, Aaron four Colorado slots. Offer two. Two choices is easy. Four is a delay.
  5. Confirm the email and the phone, and say Wade confirms today. Read both back out loud. A booking with a wrong email is not a booking.

Colorado only: Aaron is right here in the Denver metro and can come by in person. Say that. It is your strongest line, because it is true and local. Wade takes the Colorado screen share meetings if Aaron cannot.

What they might say, and the idea behind your answer

1. "What is this regarding?"

Idea: answer plainly, then ask in the same breath. Do not explain the technology.

Example, not a line to read

Wade sent the owner information about how the business shows up in AI search. I have his calendar here. I am calling to set 15 minutes for him to come by and show it. Is [decision maker] in today?

2. "Just send it again."

Idea: a yes to a resend, not a no. Take the address, then still ask for the 15 minutes.

Examples

Wade already sent it. I can have him resend it today. What is the best address?

Can I also hold 15 minutes for him next week? If the email answers everything, you can cancel.

3. "Not interested," from the front desk

Idea: the front desk is not the decision maker. Be gracious, end the call, try once more at a different time of day.

Example

Understood. Thank you. I will make a note.

4. "Not interested," from the decision maker

Idea: final. No second try, no persuading. Thank them and log it.

Example

Understood. Thank you for your time.

5. "Call me later."

Idea: a good outcome. Get a real day and a real time, and write it down.

Example

Of course. What day and what time is good for you? I will call you then.

6. "We already have a company that does this."

Idea: do not argue and do not compare. AI search is new, and most owners have never seen their own business in it. Wade shows them.

Examples

That is good to hear. This is about AI search specifically, which is very new.

Wade can show you in 15 minutes what your business looks like when somebody asks ChatGPT.

7. "How much does it cost?"

Idea: no price on this call, ever. Wade shows first and covers cost inside the 15 minutes. That is the honest answer, and it keeps the call short.

Examples

Wade goes over that with you in the meeting, after he shows you where you stand.

He wants you to see it first. That part takes 15 minutes.

8. "A visit is hard right now."

Idea: never lose a meeting over the format. Move to the screen share, same two times.

Example

Wade can do the same 15 minutes on a screen share. He shows you, and you decide. Would [slot A] or [slot B] work on a screen?

Words to use, words to avoid

Use these

  • AI search
  • shows up, does not show up
  • discoverable
  • 15 minutes
  • show you on his screen
  • ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity by name
  • Kerzie AI, when asked who you are with

Avoid these

  • SEO
  • built for
  • AI assistant
  • any price or any number
  • any promise about results or rankings
  • the date the email went out
  • "they cannot find you" (say "does not come up")

The rules in one place

  • Work the tab top to bottom. The rows are in order for a reason.
  • Skip any row Jordan already called. His callback column is filled in.
  • Three attempts, then stop. Day one morning. Day one afternoon or day two morning, at a different time of day. Day two afternoon is the last try. Never a fourth.
  • One voicemail only, on attempt two. Fifteen seconds: your name, Kerzie AI, Wade recently sent information about AI search for their business, you can set 15 minutes, your Quo number. Never a second voicemail.
  • A decision maker's no ends it. Log DM-NO. Nobody calls again.
  • A front-desk no allows one more try at a different time of day. Log GK-NO.
  • Log every attempt with a result code, including the ones nobody answered. An unlogged call never happened.
  • Honor an opt-out immediately, with no second ask.

Booking checklist

A booked row is not complete until all of this is in the sheet.

  • Result code: BOOKED-IN or BOOKED-WEB.
  • Business name.
  • Contact name, the person in the meeting.
  • Phone number, read back out loud.
  • Email address, read back out loud.
  • The day and time of the slot you booked.
  • The slot crossed off the morning list.

Tell them Wade confirms today. Wade or Aaron sends the invite the same day.
