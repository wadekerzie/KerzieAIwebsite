Released September 2026 · For every plan · **Highly recommended.** This one release
carries three earlier ones and replaces them: The Weekly Tune-Up (v1.3), Fresh Start, and
Cold Storage. Nothing in those is lost. What is new is the half of the problem nobody was
measuring, plus a hard number on the half everybody was.

> **If you are a human reading this, you are in the wrong place.** This document is
> written to your AI, not to you. Go to **kerzie.ai/downloads**, copy the install line
> for this release, and paste it into your Code tab. Your AI reads this; you do not have
> to.

**TO THE CLAUDE READING THIS - you are the installer. MEASURE, then TELL, then GO:**

1. **MEASURE BOTH HALVES, before you say anything.** What a brand-new session reads
   before the owner types a word comes from two places, and most owners have only ever
   heard about one.
   - **The repo half.** Byte and word counts on the operating manual (CLAUDE.md or
     equivalent), on the memory index if one exists, and on the tracker's open-items
     index. Add an estimated token total.
   - **The app half.** In your own system prompt right now, count the tool names carried by
     every connected app, grouped by app, and count the entries in the skills list with
     their descriptions. Estimate the tokens for each. Report the numbers.
   Do not change a single connector setting, and do not suggest disconnecting anything.
   The app half is a settings choice the owner makes, and the honest default is to leave
   connected tools connected: reconnecting them is the real cost, and the names are not
   the lever. Say that out loud so the owner is not left worrying about it.
2. **CHECK WHAT IS ALREADY IN.** Read `VERSION.md` if it exists. If it lists The Weekly
   Tune-Up, Fresh Start, or Cold Storage, name which ones in one line and keep going:
   this release folds them together, and folding is the point. If it already lists The
   Ultimate Optimizer, say so and stop.
3. **TELL the owner what changes, in plain sentences, once.** Their session start has a
   price, the repo half of it is theirs to set, and today it has no ceiling. After this
   there is a ceiling, the index becomes pointers, long reads stop happening in front of
   them, and the weekly pass reports both halves so the number never drifts again.
4. **WRITE these rules into the operating manual, in its own style.**
   - **A hard ceiling on the briefing file: 20 KB and 3,000 words.** Pointers over
     content. A line carrying a dollar figure, a date, or a phone number is a demotion
     candidate by default, because that is the kind of line that goes stale and the kind
     that belongs in a working file.
   - **Demote whole, never trim.** A section that crosses the ceiling moves VERBATIM to
     a warm file the AI opens on demand, or to the cold shelf where it stays grep-able
     and is never deleted. One pointer line stays behind.
   - **The memory index is pointers only.** Each entry is a trigger phrase of ten words
     or fewer that says WHEN to open the file, not what is inside it. "Pricing changed,
     read this" is an index line. A summary of the pricing is not.
   - **Long reads never run in the owner's own session.** Anything past roughly 150 lines
     goes to a cheap helper model that comes back with the excerpt or the answer. This is
     the rule that keeps one big morning from spending the whole week.
5. **RUN THE FIRST PASS LIVE, as one packet.** Measure, then hand the owner a single
   demotion packet with the evidence quoted and one question. One yes moves all of it.
   Never demote silently, never in pieces, and never their standing rules.
6. **SET THE WEEKLY PASS, RECORD, PROVE.** If a weekly pass already exists from The
   Weekly Tune-Up, update that one; never create a second. Otherwise schedule it however
   this OS schedules recurring work. Either way it reports BOTH halves every week, the
   repo half against the ceiling and the app half as context. Append to `VERSION.md`
   (create it if missing): `The Ultimate Optimizer - applied <today's date> - supersedes
   The Weekly Tune-Up, Fresh Start, Cold Storage`. Then show the owner the before and
   after numbers from today's pass. Removing this later means deleting the rules and the
   routine it added and noting the removal in VERSION.md.

# The Ultimate Optimizer - Both Halves of What Your Session Costs

**To the person receiving this:** if your usage keeps disappearing faster than the work
should cost, the answer is almost never your plan. It is what your AI is made to read,
and how you read with it.

Start with the part nobody explains. **Your weekly usage meter is account-wide.** A
brand-new session inherits it. It is not the same thing as how full the current
conversation is, and the two get confused constantly. Both climb from the same cause:
every turn re-sends the whole conversation, so a long session carrying a big pile of
reading is the multiplier on everything you do after it. That is why the fix is never
one clever trick. It is a ceiling, an index, and a reading habit.

**Half of your session start is yours to set.** Before you type, your AI reads your
briefing file, your memory index, and your tracker. That is the repo half, and it only
ever grows, because every session adds and almost nothing ever leaves. The other half
comes from the app: the name of every tool your connected apps carry, plus your skills
list. Worth knowing, worth measuring, and then worth leaving alone. Disconnecting things
to save on names is a trade you lose, because you pay for it in reconnecting at the worst
possible moment.

Here is what happened the day the OS this came out of got measured properly:

- The briefing file: **37.6 KB and 5,498 words, down to 20.0 KB and 2,827 words.**
- The memory index: **19.5 KB down to 9.2 KB.**
- The tracker's open-items index: **27.8 KB, about 7,100 tokens, and it did not move.**
  That one is next, and the weekly pass will show it.
- The repo half of every session start: **about 21,700 estimated tokens down to about
  14,400.**
- Nothing was deleted. The complete pre-trim briefing sits verbatim on the cold shelf.

For scale on the other half, counted the same day: **289 tool names** across the
connected apps and a skills list of **50 entries**, about **13,000 estimated tokens** all
in. Real weight. Not the lever.

So the rules this release writes in are the ones that hold the repo half down for good.
A ceiling of 20 KB and 3,000 words on the briefing, because a limit nobody wrote down is
a limit nobody keeps. Pointers instead of content, so a line with a price or a date in it
gets moved to the file that owns it. Demotions that move whole sections word for word,
never trims, because the sentence you cut is always the one you needed in November. An
index that tells your AI when to open a file instead of summarizing what is in it. And
the one that saves the most: long reads go to a cheap helper that hands back the answer,
instead of running in front of you at full price.

Then the weekly pass measures both halves and tells you the numbers, every week, whether
they moved or not. A flat week is a good week. A week where the briefing crossed the
ceiling gets you one packet and one question, and your one yes moves it all to the shelf
where nothing is ever lost.

That is the whole thing. Your AI keeps everything it has ever known, and stops carrying
all of it to work every morning.
