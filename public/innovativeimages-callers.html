What three weeks of callers got us

You asked how you'd find the companies and who to call. Here is what two part-time callers did for me, the exact play, and the prompt that builds your list. Nothing to buy from me. Read it in two minutes.

Callers earn $5 an hour plus $50 for every meeting that actually happens. The phone system (Quo, two lines with call recording) runs about $105 a month. My follow-ups after the captures are what turn emails into meetings; the callers open the door.

The play, five steps

  1. Your Claude builds the list. Paste the prompt below into your own setup. It comes back with a spreadsheet of companies near you, who to ask for, the main number, and a why-now note. You never Google a single firm.
  2. The caller works the list from a 60-second script. Ask for the person who books team photos or headshots. Get their name and email. Offer a 15-minute look at your work, in person or on a screen.
  3. Your Claude drafts one short email per capture. Your portfolio link, one line about that company, one ask. You read it and send it. Ten minutes a day.
  4. The caller calls back two days later. Warm now: "Kurt sent you a note, did you get a chance to look?" Books the 15 minutes on your calendar.
  5. You show the work. Team photo day, headshot day, the holiday party. The caller gets $50 when the meeting happens, whether or not you close.
What I would tell you from the front desk stat: 4 in 10 calls stop at reception. The script that worked for us leads with the local company name and what the call is about in one sentence, then asks for the person by role. Callers who sound like a neighbor get through more than callers who sound like a pitch.

The prompt for your Claude

Copy the block, paste it into your Claude, answer its two questions, and let it work. Change the radius or the categories if you want.

I am Kurt Nelson, Innovative Images Photography, McKinney, Texas. Thirty years shooting. I want to sell three things to local companies: corporate team events (holiday parties, retreats, awards nights), group and team photography, and professional headshots for their people.

Build me an outbound call list. Work within 25 miles of downtown McKinney, Dallas included. Ask me two questions first: how many rows I want (default 150), and any companies to leave off.

Target these company types, in this order, and tell me why each one buys photography:
1. Real estate brokerages and their offices (agent headshots, recurring as agents join)
2. Law firms with 8 to 100 attorneys (partner headshots, firm photo, website refresh)
3. Medical, dental, and orthodontic groups with 3 or more providers (staff photos for the website and the wall)
4. Financial advisors, wealth managers, banks, and credit unions (advisor headshots, branch teams)
5. CPA and insurance agencies (team pages)
6. Companies with 50 to 500 employees headquartered nearby (team events, new-hire headshots, LinkedIn photos for sales teams)
7. Home builders, mortgage lenders, and title companies (agent-facing teams that change often)
8. Private schools, churches, and universities (faculty and staff, directories, sports)
9. Hotels, country clubs, and event venues (partners who host the events I shoot)
10. The McKinney and Frisco chambers of commerce (member lists and their own events)

For each company give me: company name, type, size estimate (employees or agents), street address, main phone number, website, the title of the person who books this (Office Manager, HR Director, Marketing Director, Managing Partner, Broker-Owner), a one-line why-now note (recent move, new office, hiring surge, website with missing or mismatched headshots, anniversary), and a priority score from 1 to 5.

Sources to use: Google Maps and Places listings, the company's own website and team page, LinkedIn company pages and how many employees show no photo or an old one, the McKinney and Frisco chamber directories, the State Bar of Texas firm directory, the Dallas Business Journal lists, and local news for office moves and expansions. Only list phone numbers published by the company itself.

Deliver: a spreadsheet I can hand to a caller, sorted by priority, with the top 50 marked for week one. Then write me a 60-second call script in plain words that leads with my name and McKinney, says what the call is about in one sentence, asks for the booking person by role, and asks for their email so I can send three sample photos. Then draft the one-paragraph follow-up email I send after each capture, with a place for my portfolio link and one line about their company.

The script and the email come back in your voice if your Claude already knows it. If not, paste in two emails you have sent to clients first.
